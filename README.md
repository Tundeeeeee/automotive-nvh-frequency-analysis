# Automotive NVH — Frequency Response & Acoustic Analysis

## Project Overview

A collaborative laboratory project completed at the University of Windsor as part of MECH 8025 — Automotive NVH Laboratory.

The experiment investigated acoustic frequency response, sound pressure levels, FFT and octave-band measurements, and mechanical noise characteristics.

## Project Objectives

- Measure frequency responses using FFT and octave-band analysis.
- Compare white noise and pink noise characteristics.
- Evaluate the frequency response of large and small speakers.
- Examine sound pressure levels across different frequency ranges.
- Analyze hand-drill noise using frequency spectra.
- Estimate rotational speed using gear-mesh frequency analysis.

## Experimental Methodology

1. Conduct acoustic measurements in an anechoic room.
2. Generate white and pink noise signals.
3. Measure speaker frequency responses using FFT and octave-band analysis.
4. Compare large and small speaker performance.
5. Record hand-drill noise measurements in the CARE Shop.
6. Identify frequency peaks and gear-mesh characteristics.
7. Calculate rotational frequencies and estimate motor speed.

## Key Findings

- FFT and octave-band analysis produced different sound-level measurements.
- Larger speakers performed better at lower frequencies, while smaller speakers performed better at higher frequencies.
- Recorded white and pink noise spectra deviated from ideal theoretical patterns.
- Gear-mesh frequency analysis was used to estimate a hand-drill rotational speed of approximately 7,452 RPM.
